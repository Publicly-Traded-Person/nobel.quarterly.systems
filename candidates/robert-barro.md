**The work.** Ricardian equivalence, the new classical macro of the late 1970s, and the cross-country growth regressions that made "Barro regressions" a genre. He later put rare disasters into asset pricing.

**The case for.** He has sat on every shortlist for two decades and his citation count is enormous. Few living economists have shaped both macro theory and empirical growth.

**The case against.** He is 82, his work sprawls across too many topics for one citation, and growth got its prize in 2024 and again in 2025.

**The KmikeyM angle.** Ricardian equivalence says rational agents see through the government's financing tricks. KmikeyM shareholders see through Mike's too, which is why the books are open.
