**The work.** Increasing returns and lock-in: why an inferior technology can win a market and keep it once enough users pile on, plus the complexity economics program at Santa Fe.

**The case for.** Network effects became the operating logic of the tech industry, and Arthur wrote the theory in the 1980s when the journals did not want it.

**The case against.** Born in 1945, an engineer by training, and outside the mainstream for decades. The committee rewards work the profession absorbed, and complexity economics never quite was.

**The KmikeyM angle.** Lock-in says early adopters set the path. KmikeyM's first shareholders from 2008 are still steering, which is exactly what Arthur would predict.
