**The work.** Woodford wrote *Interest and Prices* (2003), the book that turned New Keynesian theory into the operating manual for modern central banks. Inflation targeting, forward guidance, the idea that a central bank's main tool is managing expectations rather than the money supply: the framework most central bankers now use is his, and the models on their desks descend from it.

**The case for.** Every central bank in the rich world spent the 2010s and 2020s running policy through his lens, from the zero lower bound to the inflation of 2021 and 2022. The committee rewarded the theory of central banking with Kydland and Prescott in 2004 and the practice with Bernanke in 2022; Woodford is the bridge between them. Clarivate's citation, "contributions to the theory and practice of monetary policy," reads like a prize sentence.

**The case against.** Bernanke, Diamond and Dybvig in 2022 already touched monetary economics, and the committee rarely returns to a field within five years. The New Keynesian model also took a public beating after 2021, when its inflation forecasts failed, and the committee reads the newspapers.

**The KmikeyM angle.** Woodford's insight is that a system is steered by managing what people expect it to do, not by the lever itself. That is a fair description of how KmikeyM votes actually move behavior: the announcement does most of the work before the shares trade.
