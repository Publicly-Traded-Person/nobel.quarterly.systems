**The work.** Behavioral game theory, then neuroeconomics: putting subjects in brain scanners to watch an economic choice get made.

**The case for.** He bridged psychology, game theory, and neuroscience before it was fashionable, and his book on behavioral game theory is the field's standard text.

**The case against.** Behavioral economics has been rewarded twice, with Kahneman in 2002 and Thaler in 2017, the same year Camerer was named. Neuroeconomics has not yet produced a result the committee can name.

**The KmikeyM angle.** Camerer studies how real people play strategic games with limited foresight, which is a fair description of a shareholder vote on somebody else's life.
