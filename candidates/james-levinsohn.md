**The work.** The L in BLP, and separately the Levinsohn-Petrin method for estimating production functions when firms pick inputs in response to shocks the researcher cannot see.

**The case for.** Two methods that thousands of papers cite, one on the demand side and one on the supply side of the same problem.

**The case against.** He has spent the last decade running the Jackson School at Yale rather than publishing, and if the committee rewards BLP the story will center on Berry and Pakes.

**The KmikeyM angle.** Levinsohn-Petrin corrects for the fact that a firm knows its own productivity before the analyst does. Mike knows his before his shareholders do, and gets priced anyway.
