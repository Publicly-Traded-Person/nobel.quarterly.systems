**The work.** Experimental evidence that people punish unfairness at their own expense, the Fehr-Schmidt model of inequity aversion, and the economics of reciprocity and social norms.

**The case for.** He is the most cited behavioral economist in Europe, and his experiments on altruistic punishment changed how economists think cooperation survives.

**The case against.** Behavioral got Kahneman in 2002 and Thaler in 2017. Committees hesitate to go back to the same well three times in 25 years.

**The KmikeyM angle.** Fehr's subjects pay to punish free riders. KmikeyM shareholders who spend an evening voting on how Mike lives are paying in time for a payoff no self-interest model explains, and the market runs on exactly that.
